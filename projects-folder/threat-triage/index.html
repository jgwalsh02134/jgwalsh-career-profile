      Case Study (STAR)

      Situation

      Agencies regularly receive threatening narratives—emails, tips, or social-media posts—that must be triaged quickly. Analysts need a transparent, repeatable screening method that avoids over-reliance on intuition or black-box models.

      Task

      • Design a first-line intake tool to highlight behavioral indicators.
      • Produce a banded risk score (Low/Moderate/Elevated/Critical) with suggested actions.
      • Make the process auditable and ethically framed (no over-claims).

      Action

      • Built a deterministic rules engine (JavaScript + JSON rubric) that normalizes text; scans for indicators (direct threats, weapon references, specific targets, leakage, grievance fixation); and weights them.
      • Versioned rubric.json for transparency; added an Explainability toggle to reveal the rubric.
      • Developed a live demo that outputs Score & Band, Signals Detected, and Suggested Actions.
      • Validated with a small labeled set; compared rubric to human ratings and measured inter-rater reliability.

      Result

      • Delivered a working triage prototype showing how psychology becomes structured, auditable intake.
      • Demonstrated value for federal employers (FBI, HSI, DHS, USSS) with clear limits and escalation guidance.
      • Résumé bullet: Designed a behavioral threat triage engine (10+ indicators); achieved κ ≈ 0.7 inter-rater reliability on a labeled dataset; published methods and limitations.

      Limits & Ethics

      • Indicators are signals, not proof or diagnosis.
      • Tool supports initial screening only; human review required.
      • Use public/redacted content; avoid PII in demos.
      • Document escalation criteria and preserve context.
